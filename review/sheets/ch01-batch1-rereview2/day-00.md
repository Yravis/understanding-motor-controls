**Claim (what is being checked):** The bucket control source is a 120 V, 60 Hz supply between X1 and X2 with a small internal impedance, so its terminal voltage sags a little when a coil draws inrush current. X2 is bonded to the enclosure (grounded) in this configuration.

**Control power supply (X1/X2)** (tag SRC, source)

| Parameter | Value | Unit | Tolerance/range |
|---|---|---|---|
| volts | 120 | V | ±0 |
| zsR | 0.35 | ohm | illustrative |
| zsX | 0.35 | ohm | illustrative |

Internal impedance |Zs| about 0.5 ohm is an illustrative value; real control transformers range from tenths of an ohm to several ohms depending on VA rating.

## ch01.u3.s3-10.device.led-pilot

*type* deviceSpec · *book* 3.10 p.29 (toc-unverified) · *basis* typical · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** LED pilot lights are now the most common choice. A typical 120 V unit draws on the order of 10 mA when lit. They are nonlinear: they conduct well only above a small threshold voltage, and with an ohmmeter they may read differently with the probes reversed, or OL both ways, depending on the internal circuit.

**120 V LED pilot light** (tag LED, led)

| Parameter | Value | Unit | Tolerance/range |
|---|---|---|---|
| rLamp | 12000 | ohm | typical 8k-20k |
| vOnRms | 3 | V | illustrative |
| vf | 1.8 | V | illustrative |
| litI | 0.002 | A | illustrative; visibly lit threshold, well below the 10 mA running current |
| rOff | 470000 | ohm | illustrative; off-state AC impedance |

Lit: 120 V / 12 kohm = 10 mA. Off: still a high but finite impedance (470 kohm in this model), so a 10 Mohm meter across an open contact feeding the lamp reads about 115 V (near line), while a 3 kohm LoZ meter draws enough current to turn the LED on and reads about 24 V (120 x 3k / 15k). The ohmmeter model conducts forward only (diode); real LED pilot lights vary by manufacturer, and many with a bridge rectifier or driver read OL both ways.

*Reviewer notes (builder's doubts):* Typical values only; a real datasheet is needed before any value is graded.
