# ch01-batch1-rereview3 · Day 9 - Reading a motor control schematic

## ch01.u3.s3-18.fault.contact-on-x2-side

*type* fault · *book* 3.18 p.37 (toc-unverified) · *basis* illustrative · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** START pushbutton PB1 was wired between the CR1 coil and X2 instead of between the E-stop and the coil. The circuit still works, but while START is released with the E-stop reset, both CR1 coil terminals sit at X1 potential, so a ground fault on the field wire between CR1 and PB1 would energize CR1 without pressing START (the arrangement NEC 2026 430.74 prohibits in a motor control circuit).

**START pushbutton wired on the X2 side of CR1**

*Cause:* START pushbutton PB1 was wired between the CR1 coil and X2 instead of between the E-stop and the coil. The circuit still works, but while START is released with the E-stop reset, both CR1 coil terminals sit at X1 potential, so a ground fault on the field wire between CR1 and PB1 would energize CR1 without pressing START (the arrangement NEC 2026 430.74 prohibits in a motor control circuit).

*Symptom:* Operator: "Everything works." (Behavior is unchanged: a hazard found by measurement. While START is released with the E-stop reset, both CR1 coil terminals read 120 V to ground instead of 0 V.)

*Fix:* reland W:PB1:1: Land PB1 on the X1 side (wire 3, after the E-stop).; reland W:PB1:2: Land the PB1 output on wire 4.; reland W:CR1:C1: Land the CR1 coil feed on wire 4.; reland W:CR1:C2: Land CR1 directly on X2 (wire 2).

*Reviewer notes (builder's doubts):* Expected readings are generated by the model (see review sheets); device values are typical/illustrative. Check that the symptom matches what the model shows and that the fix restores behavior.

## ch01.u3.s3-18.fault.x1-ground-fault-high-impedance

*type* fault · *book* 3.18 p.37 (toc-unverified) · *basis* illustrative · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** The same X1 conductor touches the enclosure through a high-impedance path (damp, damaged insulation). Current is too small to open the fuse: a hazard.

**X1 high-impedance ground fault (does NOT clear)**

*Cause:* The same X1 conductor touches the enclosure through a high-impedance path (damp, damaged insulation). Current is too small to open the fuse: a hazard.

*Symptom:* Operator: "No problem, everything runs." (A hazard: the fault does not open the fuse and is found by measurement. After lockout, pull F1 or lift the lead before an ohms check; with F1 in, the transformer winding reads about 0.4 Ω from the X1-side wiring to ground and will hide a high-impedance path.)

*Fix:* remove GF: Repair the damaged conductor.
