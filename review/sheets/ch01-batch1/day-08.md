## ch01.u3.s3-14.card.pneumatic-timer

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.14 p.35 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** A pneumatic timing relay is coil driven and delays by metering air through an adjustable opening.

**Pneumatic timing relays**

A pneumatic timing relay is coil operated. When the coil energizes, a mechanism moves while air is metered through an adjustable opening, so the timed contacts change only after the set delay. Turning the adjustment changes the delay. Because air movement is affected by wear, dirt, and temperature, repeatability is looser than for electronic timers. The coil itself is the timing input, so no separate supply is needed. An on-delay model starts timing when the coil is energized, and an off-delay model starts timing when the coil is de-energized.

*Field note:* A pneumatic timer that drifts or gets slower may have a blocked orifice or aged parts, not a bad coil.

## ch01.u3.s3-15.card.instantaneous-contacts

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.15 p.35 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** Instantaneous contacts on a timing relay change with the coil immediately, with no delay.

**Instantaneous contacts on a timing relay**

Many timing relays include both timed contacts and instantaneous contacts. Instantaneous contacts follow the coil without any delay: they change state as soon as the coil energizes and return as soon as it de-energizes. Timed contacts follow the delay rules. When tracing a circuit, look at which contact set the schematic uses. A contact marked with a timing symbol waits; an unmarked contact on the same device does not. Using the wrong set can make a lamp or load switch at the wrong time even though the timer itself is working.

*Field note:* If a load switches at the wrong moment, confirm that the timed contact set, not the instantaneous set, is wired to it.

## ch01.u3.s3-16.card.solid-state-timer

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.16 p.36 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** A solid-state off-delay timer needs a constant supply plus a separate trigger, and losing supply drops the output immediately.

**Solid-state timing relays**

A solid-state timing relay uses electronics to time the delay, with an adjustable setting and generally better repeatability than a pneumatic timer. An on-delay solid-state timer can time from the moment its supply is applied. An off-delay solid-state timer works differently: it needs constant supply power to stay alive, plus a separate trigger input. The trigger energizing makes the output transfer immediately, and removing the trigger starts the delay. If the constant supply is lost, the output drops at once, with no timing. In the reference bucket, TD4 has its supply on wire 1 and its trigger on wire 13.

*Field note:* A solid-state off-delay wired without a constant supply will not time out; it just drops out when the supply is gone.

## ch01.u3.s3-17.card.on-delay-off-delay

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.17 p.36 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** On-delay contacts are NOTC and NCTO; off-delay contacts are NOTO and NCTC; interrupted timing resets.

**On-delay vs off-delay and the contact names**

On-delay timers start timing when energized. Their NO contact is NOTC: normally open, timed closed, closing after the delay. Their NC contact is NCTO: normally closed, timed open. When the coil de-energizes, the contacts return at once. Off-delay timers transfer immediately on energize and start timing when de-energized. Their NO contact is NOTO and their NC contact is NCTC. If the coil drops before an on-delay finishes, it resets and the contacts never change. If the coil returns during an off-delay, it resets and the contacts stay transferred.

*Field note:* Read the contact name left to right: normal state, then timed action. NOTC means open, then closes after timing.

## ch01.u3.s3-17.card.timer-reset-rules

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.17 p.36 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** On-delay timing resets if the coil drops early, and off-delay timing resets if the coil returns during timing.

**Timer reset rules**

Three rules cover most timing questions. First, an on-delay resets to zero if its coil drops before the preset, so the contacts never change state. Second, an off-delay transfers its contacts the moment the coil energizes, and timing begins on de-energize. Third, if an off-delay coil is re-energized while it is timing, the timer resets and the contacts remain in the energized state. A solid-state off-delay times on its trigger, but only while its supply is present. A scenario that ends exactly at the preset counts as timed out.

*Field note:* When a timed lamp flickers or never lights, trace whether the coil input was interrupted before the delay finished.
