## ch01.u3.s3-10.card.pushbutton-types

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.10 p.29 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** A pushbutton's normal state is unpressed; momentary types spring back and maintained types stay put until operated again.

**Pushbuttons: head styles and momentary vs maintained**

A pushbutton is an operator-actuated pilot device with one or more contact blocks. Common heads are flush (hard to press by accident), extended, and the large mushroom head. Momentary buttons spring back when released, so their contacts return to the normal state. Maintained buttons stay in the pressed position until operated again (push-pull and twist-to-release styles are common). Normal always means the unpressed, shelf state: a normally open button closes when pressed, a normally closed button opens when pressed. A START function is usually a momentary NO button; a STOP function is usually an NC button.

*Field note:* When a button seems to do the opposite of its label, check the contact block type and whether the head is maintained before blaming the wiring.

## ch01.u3.s3-10.card.mushroom-estop

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.10 p.29 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** A mushroom-head stop button is typically a maintained, normally closed device that latches when pressed.

**The mushroom head as a stop device**

The large mushroom head is easy to strike quickly with the palm, even with a gloved or full hand, which is why it is a common choice for emergency-stop style buttons. Many such heads are maintained: once struck they latch in the pressed position and must be deliberately reset by twisting or pulling. The contact block behind a stop head is normally closed, so pressing it opens the circuit and de-energizes the coil. In the reference bucket, PB3 is a maintained mushroom NC stop in series with the PB1 START button on the rung-1 path to the CR1 coil.

*Field note:* A latched stop button is a clue when a machine will not restart: someone may have pressed it and nobody reset it.

## ch01.u3.s3-11.card.solenoid-coil-load

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.11 p.31 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** A solenoid is a coil load that pulls in a plunger, and an AC solenoid draws more current while the plunger is out than when seated.

**A solenoid is a coil that moves a plunger**

A solenoid is an electromagnet coil with a movable iron plunger. When the coil is energized, the magnetic field pulls the plunger in, and the plunger moves a valve, latch, or lever. On the schematic a solenoid is a load, so it connects on the X2 side, with its controlling contacts on the X1 side, just like a relay coil. An AC solenoid draws a much larger current while the plunger is out than after it seats, the same inrush idea as a relay coil. Qualitatively, that is why a plunger that jams open can overheat the coil.

*Field note:* A solenoid valve that hums but does not move is a warning sign: stop and find the mechanical cause before the coil cooks.

## ch01.u3.s3-12.card.nohc-ncho

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.12 p.31 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** NOHC means normally open on the shelf but held closed in the installation; NCHO is the reverse.

**Switch operations: held-closed and held-open contacts**

The shelf state of a switch is its contact arrangement with nothing acting on it. In a real installation a machine part may be pressing the actuator all the time. A contact that is normally open on the shelf but held closed by the machine in its resting position is described as normally open, held closed (NOHC). A contact that is normally closed on the shelf but held open by the machine is normally closed, held open (NCHO). The symbol shows the shelf state with a note or marking for the held condition, so read both before predicting what the circuit sees.

*Field note:* When a limit switch sits against a machine at rest, the circuit sees the held state, not the shelf state.

## ch01.u3.s3-12.card.normal-vs-installed

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.12 p.31 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** Normal refers to the shelf state, and the installed state also depends on what acts on the actuator.

**Normal is the shelf state, not the installed state**

Throughout controls, normal means de-energized and not actuated, the state as manufactured and shipped. It does not describe what the device is doing in the installed system. A pressure switch bolted to a pressurized line is actuated even though its contacts are still called normally open. The same idea explains held-closed and held-open contacts. To predict a circuit, take the shelf state, then apply whatever the installation is doing to the actuator right now. Skipping the second step is a very common source of wrong predictions and wrong troubleshooting conclusions.

*Field note:* Before meter work, ask two questions: what is the shelf state, and what is acting on the actuator right now?

## ch01.u3.s3-13.card.temperature-switch

*type* lessonCard · *book* 3.13 p.34 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** A temperature switch's normal state is its shelf state; installed in a hot process it may already be actuated, and it may have a deadband.

**Temperature switches: shelf state vs installed**

A temperature switch changes its contacts when the sensed temperature crosses a setpoint. Its normal state is the shelf state at rest, before it senses a process condition. A normally open temperature switch is open when it is not actuated, which for a heating-limit style device means below its setpoint, and closes when the sensed temperature rises past the setpoint. Many have a deadband, so the contacts return only after the temperature falls some distance below the actuation point. The sensor reading and the switch contacts are different things, so an installed switch may already be actuated by a hot process.

*Field note:* If a high-temperature lamp is lit, check the actual process temperature before suspecting the switch.

*Format* mcq · *Day* 7

**What separates a maintained pushbutton from a momentary one?**

- ✅ It stays in the operated position until it is reset
- ❌ It has more contact blocks than a momentary button  
  *why wrong:* The number of contact blocks does not decide whether a button latches.

**Claim (what is being checked):** A large mushroom head is a common stop-button choice because it is easy to strike quickly.

*Format* mcq · *Day* 7

**Why is the large mushroom head a common choice for stop and emergency-stop buttons?**

- ✅ It is easy to hit quickly with a palm or glove
- ❌ It makes the contact block carry more current  
  *why wrong:* The head shape does not change the contact rating.
- ❌ It prevents the button from being pressed by accident, like a flush head  
  *why wrong:* The flush head is the accident-resistant style; the mushroom head is the easy-to-hit style.

  *why wrong:* A pilot device needs a circuit to operate; head shape does not change that.

*Explanation:* A stop device must be reachable fast, even in a hurry or with a gloved or full hand. The big head is easy to strike and is hard to miss, while small flush heads are intended to avoid accidental operation.

## ch01.u3.s3-11.question.solenoid-jammed-plunger

*type* question · *book* 3.11 p.31 (toc-unverified) · *basis* general-knowledge · *alignment* provisional

**Claim (what is being checked):** An AC solenoid draws more current while its plunger is out, so a plunger that jams open can overheat the coil.

*Format* mcq · *Day* 7

**An AC solenoid is energized but its plunger is jammed open and never seats. What is the likely consequence?**

- ✅ The coil keeps drawing the higher current and can overheat
- ❌ The coil current falls to zero because the plunger is out  
  *why wrong:* The plunger being out raises current, not lowers it.
- ❌ The coil switches itself off with its own contacts  
  *why wrong:* A plain solenoid has no internal contacts that turn it off.
